Ireland

President Connolly signs the Regulation of Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026 into law

President Catherine Connolly signed the Regulation of Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026 on 21 July 2026, the final step after it completed Seanad Report and Final Stage on 15 July — Ireland's domestic AI Act enforcement architecture is now law, not just a bill in passage. The Act establishes Oifig IS na hÉireann (AI Office of Ireland) as the coordinating authority and single point of contact for the EU AI Act, on track for its 1 August 2026 operational deadline. Practical effect for anyone advising Irish-facing AI deployments: the 15-regulator sectoral model under S.I. 366/2025 (Central Bank, DPC, CCPC etc. as Market Surveillance Authorities) now has its statutory backbone in force, and the AI Office's coordination, registry and sandbox functions are no longer contingent on the Bill passing — they are a live legal obligation with 11 days to go before the Article 50 deadline.

Source: President of Ireland (Áras an Uachtaráin)

France

France's competition authority: three firms hold 84% of the AI agents market

The Autorité de la concurrence published Opinion 26-A-05 on 17 July 2026, its third AI market study after cloud computing and generative AI, this time examining agentic AI. Headline finding: OpenAI, Google and Anthropic together hold over 84% of the AI agent market, with lock-in risk compounding as agents become the primary interface between users and the wider digital economy — vertically integrated players who bundle agents into existing products (OS, browser, messaging) gain a structural advantage, and agentic commerce could send AI agents 20-25% of e-commerce traffic by 2030. The Authority stopped short of recommending new AI-specific rules, instead pushing for full use of existing competition law, the AI Act and the DMA, plus interoperability and data-portability commitments from operators. For any client building or procuring agentic AI tools, this is the first authority-level market-concentration data point on the sector — useful context alongside the CNIL/CIANum note on agentic AI and GDPR from three days earlier (20 July, already tracked), both signals that French regulators are treating agent autonomy as this year's live compliance and competition frontier.

Source: Autorité de la concurrence
